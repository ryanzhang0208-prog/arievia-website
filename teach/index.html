Inside Stablecoins, Used Safely

Written for older New Yorkers, their families, and the people who help them. We don't tell anyone to buy or use stablecoins. We teach them how to check.

  1. What a stablecoin is, and is not
  2. The new federal law
  3. Five checks before you decide
  4. Protect your access and send carefully
  5. How scammers use stablecoins
  6. Words that should stop you
  7. If something goes wrong, and where to get help

Already sent money or crypto to a scammer? Stop sending, and contact your bank or the app you used right away. Then report it to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center and the Federal Trade Commission.

Built to work alongside federal materials

Free federal resources already cover many scams: the FTC's Pass It On has 14 topics, and the FDIC's Money Smart for Older Adults has 10 modules. As of October 2026, none of them is dedicated to cryptocurrency or stablecoins. Our kit is designed to be taught alongside them and adds that piece: how to check whether a payment stablecoin's issuer is approved under the GENIUS Act, what redemption and reserve disclosures to look for, and the red flags of stablecoin scams, in English and Simplified Chinese.

Arievia is not affiliated with or endorsed by the FTC or the FDIC.

How we work

We teach, we don't sell

We don't issue, endorse, or promote any token or digital-asset product. We teach people how to check a product, not which one to buy.

Independent

We name every supporter, and no supporter writes or reviews our materials. Independent advisers review every guide before we publish it.

Free, and in your language

Our guides, workshop kit, and community workshops are free, in English and Chinese, and open for libraries, schools, and community groups to reuse.
