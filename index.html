Money is changing. Everyone should understand how.

Arievia Foundation is a New York nonprofit. We teach how stablecoins and tokenized assets work, and how to use them safely, in English and Chinese.

What we do Partner with us

Our mission

To prepare users, founders, and institutions to take part safely in the stablecoin and tokenized-asset economy, through public education.

Three goals, each built on the one before

  • 1Teach the public

    Help people use regulated stablecoins and tokenized assets safely and recognize scams. Senior centers, libraries, community banks, and other organizations can teach with our free materials.

  • 2Teach the builders

    Cohorts, mentorship, and hackathons for students, early-career professionals, and founders, teaching them to build fraud protection and compliance into their products.

  • 3Shape the guides others follow

    Turn what works in our programs into public best practices for fraud prevention, KYC/AML design, and responsible adoption, and bring them into the guidance, standards, and rules that others follow, through public comments and published evaluation results.

Why now

The United States has written rules for dollar stablecoins. Most people haven't learned them yet.

  1. July 18, 2025

    The GENIUS Act becomes law, setting the first federal rules for payment stablecoins.

  2. By January 18, 2027

    The law takes effect. From then on, only approved issuers may issue payment stablecoins in the U.S.

  3. July 18, 2028

    U.S. exchanges and apps generally may offer only stablecoins from approved issuers.

New rules protect the people who know them. In 2025, fraud complaints to the FBI involving cryptocurrency reported more than $11 billion in losses. Source: FBI, 2025 Internet Crime Report.

What we do

Four kinds of programs that share one set of free, bilingual materials.

  • Workshops, talks, and roundtables

    Free community workshops at libraries, senior centers, and campuses, and talks and roundtables in New York with practitioners from finance, technology, and policy. Plain language, from the basics up.

    First public program held in February 2026. First community workshop planned for early 2027.

  • Online learning

    Guides, recorded talks, webinars, and short explainers in English and Chinese, so people anywhere in the country can learn, and other organizations can teach with them.

    First guide planned for winter 2026–27.

  • Cohorts with mentors

    Two small-group tracks, each with mentors: one for students and early-career professionals, and one for founders. In the founder track, our Founder Lab, early-stage teams work through a plain-language checklist for building safer digital-finance products. No equity, no token launches.

    First Founder Lab planned for 2027.

  • Hackathons

    Hackathons with universities and technology partners, where teams build tools that protect users: scam warnings, safer wallet recovery, and payment checks. No token launches or token prizes.

    Planned for 2028–29.

Our team

Ryan Zhang

Founder and President

Ryan has spent his career on payment and fraud-prevention systems. At Goldman Sachs he was a senior product manager working on fraud and risk systems in the consumer business, and before that, as a consultant with USAA's digital banking and payments teams, he added authentication and transaction controls to wire transfers and phone banking.
